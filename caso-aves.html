The idea

Teaching how attention is researched often stays theoretical. This project turns the process into a game: participants look for birds in the jungle while the team records where they look, so the stages of an eye-tracking study are practiced in a concrete way.

The birds and the score

Each scene has birds in three categories, two colors each. The score is the fixation time multiplied by the value of each bird; only fixations of at least 0.2 seconds count.

Penalty · −5 per second

Red and magenta, the most eye-catching. They assess inhibitory control: resisting what grabs attention.

Reward · +5 per second

Yellow and orange. They assess brief visual search.

High reward · +10 per second

Olive green and brown, the camouflaged birds that are hardest to find. They assess active, goal-directed search.
